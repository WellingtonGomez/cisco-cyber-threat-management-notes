# Module 1.2 - The Ethics of Cybersecurity

## Overview

This module introduced cybersecurity ethics, cybercrime classifications, and major laws and regulations that govern privacy, security, and data protection.

---

## Ethics in Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity professionals must make ethical decisions while balancing legal requirements, organizational interests, and individual rights.

### Ethical Approaches

* **Utilitarian Ethics** – Actions are ethical if they create the greatest good for the greatest number of people.
* **Rights Approach** – Decisions should respect individual rights such as privacy, safety, and fairness.
* **Common Good Approach** – Ethical actions benefit the entire community and support shared goals.

---

## Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics

1. Do not use a computer to harm others.
2. Do not interfere with other people's computer work.
3. Do not access files without permission.
4. Do not use a computer to steal.
5. Do not spread false information.
6. Do not use software without proper licensing.
7. Do not use computer resources without authorization.
8. Respect intellectual property.
9. Consider the social impact of technology.
10. Use technology with respect and consideration for others.

## Types of Cybercrime

### Computer-Targeted Crime

The computer is the target of the attack.

**Examples:**

* Malware
* Hacking
* Denial-of-Service (DoS)

### Computer-Assisted Crime

A computer is used to commit a crime.

**Examples:**

* Fraud
* Identity theft
* Financial scams

### Computer-Incidental Crime

A computer contains information related to a crime but is not the primary tool used.

---

## U.S. Cybersecurity Laws

### Statutory Law

Laws created by Congress.

**Example:**

* Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)

### Administrative Law

Rules enforced by government agencies.

**Examples:**

* Federal Communications Commission (FCC)
* Federal Trade Commission (FTC)

### Common Law

Legal precedents established through court decisions.

---

## Federal Information Security Management Act (FISMA)

Federal agencies must maintain information security programs that include:

* Risk assessments
* IT system inventories
* Security policies and procedures
* Security awareness training
* Incident response planning
* Continuity planning

---

## Industry Regulations

### GLBA

Protects customer financial information.

### SOX

Improves financial reporting and corporate accountability.

### PCI DSS

Protects payment card information and reduces fraud.

### Cryptography Regulations

Controls the import and export of encryption technologies.

---

## Privacy and Data Protection Laws

### Privacy Act of 1974

Protects personal information maintained by federal agencies.

### FOIA

Provides public access to government records.

### FERPA

Protects student educational records.

### COPPA

Protects online privacy of children under 13.

### CIPA

Protects minors from harmful internet content.

### VPPA

Protects video rental and viewing history.

### HIPAA

Protects healthcare information and patient privacy.

### SB 1386

Requires notification when personal data breaches occur.

---

## Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)

A PIA helps organizations ensure Personally Identifiable Information (PII) is properly protected.

### PIA Process

1. Define scope
2. Identify stakeholders
3. Document PII handling
4. Review legal requirements
5. Identify compliance gaps
6. Review findings

---

## International Cybersecurity Efforts

### Convention on Cybercrime

An international treaty that addresses cybercrime, fraud, copyright violations, and network security offenses.

### EPIC

A nonprofit organization that promotes privacy rights and open government policies.

---

## Key Takeaways

* Cybersecurity professionals must follow ethical principles and legal requirements.
* Ethical decision-making often requires balancing multiple perspectives.
* Cybercrime continues to grow globally.
* Privacy laws help protect sensitive personal information.
* Organizations must implement policies and procedures to ensure compliance and data protection.
